# The food universe — Phase 18 W1

`lib/foods/universe/manifest.ts` is the curated, versioned list of generic whole and minimally
processed foods an open food world may draw from (`docs/specs/phase-18-open-world-foods.md` D1,
slice W1). This document says what is in it, how each field is derived, and how a change ships.

## Status: inert

Nothing in the app reads the universe. It is admitted for generation only when the W2b open-world
gate opens, and that gate stays off until the owner's review of the current version is recorded in
its receipt (`docs/catalog/universe/<version>.md`). `tests/foods/universe-data.test.ts` fails if any
module under `app/`, `components/` or `lib/` (outside `lib/foods/universe/`) imports it.

## What is in it

| Layer | Source | Count |
|---|---|---|
| Base | The generic staples of `lib/food-db.json`, offered to every cuisine | 256 |
| Cuisine-pack staples | Commonly eaten foods of the 12 packs that the base did not carry, offered through their packs only | 63 |

The base leaves out D1's groups (sweets, drinks, condiments, the supplement and the prepared meal
rows), every label row (no SR Legacy identity), and the reviewed exclusions in
`lib/foods/universe/data/excluded.ts`: cured and processed meats, breaded or formed foods, packaged
snacks, fried potatoes, prepared dishes and baked goods, sauces and dressings, sweetened dairy,
mixes and baby food, the whey supplement, raw foods that need cooking (raw cassava, raw egg),
flavoured or sweetened products (ramen soup, coconut milk beverage, mayonnaise, sweetened dried
cranberries and coconut), rows that are not one plain identity (the plain bagel row includes
sesame and onion variants; "vegetable oil" is soybean oil), five branded SR rows (Chobani, Silk, General Mills,
Ralston, Cream of Wheat), and cherry tomatoes, which share their SR row with raw tomato.

The 63 pack staples were added to the curated `UNIVERSE_STAPLES` list in
`scripts/build-food-db.py`, and `lib/food-db.json` was regenerated by that script from the local
USDA SR Legacy backup (`~/Backups/usda/sr_legacy/`, the 2018-04 CSV release). The JSON is never
hand-edited. The regeneration also adds `fdcId` (the `fdc_sr_legacy` catalog `source_id`) and
`srCategory` (the SR food group, which the catalog stores as the revision's `category`) to every
SR row. Every food already bundled keeps its values and flags: `FLAG_OVERRIDES` in the script now
carries the off-plan flags that audit 2026-09-13 (#23, #28) had patched into the JSON by hand.

### Curated fields (typed by a person)

Per entry (`lib/foods/universe/seed.ts`): the bundled food id, its SR Legacy FoodData Central id
(pinned: a regenerated JSON that resolves the food to another SR row fails the data test), reviewed
sensory tags from the fixed list in `vocabulary.ts`, and `countable` (false only for the herbs and
citrus that season rather than feed; such an item never counts toward the taste profile and never
fills a plate part). Cuisine tiers come from the 12 packs in `data/packs.ts`.

**Cuisine vocabulary (12):** American; Mexican and Latin American; Italian; Mediterranean and Middle
Eastern; Indian and South Asian; Chinese; Japanese; Korean; Southeast Asian; Caribbean; African;
European (other). `CUISINE_PAIRING_GROUP` maps each onto the six pairing groups of
`lib/meals/data/cuisine.ts` (African onto `global`). Tiers are `core`, `common` and `occasional`;
each pack tiers 30 to 60 items. A tier is a ranking signal for a stated cuisine (D8). It never admits
or excludes a food.

### Derived fields (never typed)

`lib/foods/universe/derive.ts`:

- **Role and parts** from `lib/foods/role.ts` and `lib/meals/parts.ts`, over per-gram macros.
- **`policy_categories`** from `lib/catalog/category-map.ts` over the SR description and food group,
  exactly as `scripts/annotate-policy-categories.ts` reads the catalog revision. `[]` means checked
  and clean; it is never NULL.
- **Allergen state** for every key in `ALLERGEN_RULES`, from deterministic rules only (never Jev):
  `listed` when the curated name or the SR description names the allergen; `clear` when nothing
  names it and the whole-food class rules it out; otherwise a declared `unknown`. An `unknown`
  item stays out of generation for an account that avoids that allergen.

The whole-food class reads the curated name, passed to the policy as `wholeFoodName`. SR
descriptions carry survey qualifiers ("Beans, black, mature seeds, cooked, boiled, without salt")
that the class table does not know, so read alone they would clear only 67 items; the curated names
clear 134. The 72 items cleared by the curated name alone are listed in the review receipt. The
curated name is not read when the SR description discloses what the name hides (an additive, a
preparation, sweetening, fortification): 9 items, among them cooked shrimp ("may contain additives
to retain moisture") and edamame ("frozen, prepared"), clear nothing by class.
Escalation reads both names, and every name-based `listed` is also a listing of the SR description,
so the stored evidence the annotate script writes carries all of them. `universePolicyEvidence`
hands `evaluatePolicy` these same inputs; the data test checks that the two agree for every item and
allergen. The W2b candidate reader must pass the curated name the same way.

## Versioning

`UNIVERSE_VERSION` (semver) and `UNIVERSE_HASH` (sha256 over every curated field and the bundled row
each entry reads: name, SR row, food group, nutrition). Both enter the meal snapshot's `inputsHash`
(W8). A change of content changes the hash, and the data test fails until the version moves and a
receipt `docs/catalog/universe/<version>.md` names the reviewer, the hash and the diff.

## Annotating the catalog

    pnpm exec tsx scripts/annotate-universe.ts                         # read-only report
    pnpm exec tsx scripts/annotate-universe.ts --commit                # a branch or local database
    pnpm exec tsx scripts/annotate-universe.ts --commit --production   # owner step only

The guard is `scripts/food-membership-audit.ts`'s, with `--commit` for `--fix`. Per item the script
first checks the identity: exactly one active `fdc_sr_legacy` revision for the pinned id, with the
bundled row's description and food group. It then writes `policy_categories` where the stored array
differs, and allergen evidence through `recordAllergenEvidence` (the warm path's writer: never a
downgrade, Jev holds kept). It has not been run against any database in W1.

## The cuisine × rule-profile matrix

`tests/foods/universe-matrix-{a,b,c,d}.test.ts`: 12 cuisines (each with the base) × 8 rule
profiles × 7 dates, 4-meal days at 2,000 kcal and 140 g protein, through `lib/meals` `planDay`. The
pool is each cuisine's admitted items: `evaluatePolicy` says `allowed` under deterministic evidence,
nutrition is usable, and the item is countable. The profiles: none, vegetarian, vegan, gluten-free,
dairy-free, peanut and tree-nut allergy, shellfish and fish allergy, and no pork (the halal food
rule; the `halal` flag itself needs certification evidence, which no universe item has, so it
admits nothing automatically). **All 12 cuisines pass all 672 days with a plate in every slot.** The
vegan days miss the 35 g protein target on 3 of 28 slots per cuisine (a plate is still served).

## Findings for the owner's review

- About 40 % of items clear each allergen. The rest are `unknown`, mostly because the whole-food
  class table (`lib/policy/whole-food-classes.ts`) does not list them (okra, bok choy, most leafy
  greens, breads, cheeses). Widening that table is a safety change for its own review, not W1's.
- Fixed fail-closed listings (W022, 2026-10-10): tahini read as dairy ("sesame butter" was not in
  the dairy exclusions of `lib/policy/allergens.ts`) and coconut milk read as tree nut (its SR row
  opens with the food-group head "Nuts,"). Both now read `unknown` for that allergen, not `listed`:
  still out of generation for an account that avoids it, never cleared
  (`tests/policy/allergen-name-false-positives.test.ts`). The category map still files yardlong
  beans, an edible-pod vegetable, under `beans`; that is a `policy_categories` change for its own
  review and an annotate re-run.
- Some curated names are looser than their SR rows: jasmine and basmati rice resolve to generic
  long-grain rows, duck breast to whole duck meat, pulled pork to roasted shoulder, sautéed onion to
  boiled onion, mixed greens to green-leaf lettuce. Nutrition is close and no allergen hides in the
  difference; the owner decides whether to rename them.
- Lemon and lime are `countable: false`: they are eaten as a squeeze of juice far more than as fruit.
- The cuisine tiers and sensory tags were proposed by the builder, not measured (D1: commonness is
  measured only by the W1b prior).
